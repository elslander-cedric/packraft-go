# Glossary

Shared language for packraft-go. One term per entry: what it means *in this project*.

- **Packrafting**: Travelling through the backcountry by combining hiking with paddling an inflatable, backpack-sized boat (a packraft).
- **Packraft**: A lightweight inflatable boat that folds small enough to carry in a backpack.
- **Trip**: A planned packrafting journey, usually multi-day, made of one or more legs.
- **Leg**: The part of a trip between two consecutive points, in one mode: a *hike leg* (on foot, boat carried) or a *paddle leg* (on water). Where a leg starts and ends comes from where you placed the points; the unit you think in is usually a section.
- **Loop**: A trip that ends where it starts: its last point is on its start point. *Back to start* makes a trip a loop by adding a leg from the last point to the start. After that the start and the end are separate points; moving one doesn't move the other.
- **Put-in**: The point where a hike leg ends and a paddle leg begins; you inflate and launch.
- **Take-out**: The point where a paddle leg ends and a hike leg begins; you get off the water and pack up.
- **Day**: The part of a trip between the start (or a camp) and the next camp (or the end). A one-day trip has a single day and no camps.
- **Camp**: An overnight stop marked on the route. Camps split a trip into days.
- **Portage**: Carrying the packraft around an obstacle on the water (e.g. a weir). Modelled as a short hike leg between two paddle legs.
- **Lock**: A chamber that lifts boats between two water levels on a navigable waterway. Paddlers don't pass locks on the water: they portage (Flanders bans it outright; in Brussels no rule lets small craft in). Locks in Flanders and Brussels are marked on the access layer from OpenStreetMap.
- **Drop**: The elevation lost from a paddle leg's put-in to its take-out. Shown instead of climb/descent on paddle legs. A paddle section's drop runs from its first put-in to its last take-out, so it is a net figure, not the sum of its legs' drops.
- **Navigable waterway**: A canal or river officially open to shipping (in Flanders managed by De Vlaamse Waterweg). Access rules differ from those for non-navigable rivers. See `docs/research/access-rules.md`.
- **Obstacle**: A fixed thing on a Walloon river that the SPW marks: a dam, an impassable point, rocks, a drossage or a low bridge (the SPW's *objet particulier*). Obstacles ship with the app and work offline. A paddle leg that passes one gets a warning, which never blocks and never changes the access status. No obstacle list is complete.
- **Incident**: A temporary hazard the SPW reports on a river, such as works or a power station outflow. Incidents come live, so they need signal. A paddle leg that passes one gets a warning with the SPW's text. An incident never changes the access status, even when its text says the river is closed. It applies until its expiry date, or to every date if it has none.
- **Stretch**: A legally defined section of river where paddling is regulated as one unit (e.g. Walloon AGW Annex 1 stretches), with its own season, hours and flow thresholds.
- **Official stretch**: A river stretch as the SPW publishes it on its kayak site, e.g. `SEMOIS4-5`. There are 21. Not always the same as an AGW Annex 1 stretch: the boundaries can differ, and some Annex 1 stretches have no official stretch at all, so they have no official status.
- **Aire**: A designated put-in or take-out point on a Walloon stretch (*aire d'embarquement / de débarquement*). In Wallonia you may only get in or out at an aire. The app knows the aires the SPW publishes (official aires) and any you've marked yourself as an access point of kind official aire.
- **Official aire**: An aire the SPW publishes, shipped with the app so it works offline. It has a use (put-in, take-out or both), which the aire warning honours. It's read-only and is not one of your access points. Not the same as an access point of kind official aire, which is your own mark.
- **Access status**: Whether water may be paddled at a given moment. One of: **open**; **closed today** (legal water, but closed by season, hours or flow, or by the official status); **forbidden** (an explicit ban, e.g. a port or excluded canal); **unknown** (no rule or no data, or an official status code the app doesn't know).
- **Official status**: The SPW's own status for an official stretch today, read live from its service. It carries what the app can't work out, such as a ministerial order or a closure made by hand. For today it wins over the status the app computes, except that a calendar closure (season, hours, fishing day) still counts. If the SPW can't be reached, the computed status stands and the app says official closures weren't checked. It's never known for a future date: the status there stays rule-based, and a stretch closed today says so in a note. If the SPW can't be reached, a future date says official closures weren't checked too.
- **Access point**: A place you've marked where you can get onto or off the water: a put-in, a take-out, or both. Its kind is official aire, slipway, jetty or informal bank. It has a name so you can recognise it in a list: anything you like ("Hotton bridge", "R3"), not necessarily unique, or else its default name. Access points belong to you, not to a trip, and any trip can use them. Being an access point doesn't make it legal; see Aire. The SPW's official aires are not access points; see Official aire.
- **Access point editing**: The planner's mode for changing your access points: marking, moving, editing and deleting them. To mark one, tap the map to place a new access point (the *draft* in code), drag it into place, then the flag button opens its details; it's stored only when you save them. While in it the trip can't be changed; outside it your access points can't be changed and don't react on the map, though snap offers still move trip points onto them. You're always outside it when the app opens. *Avoid*: "unlocked".
- **Default name**: The name the app gives an access point from the nearest road and place, e.g. "Rue du Moulin, Hotton". It follows the point when you move it, until you type a name of your own.
- **Place search**: Finding a place by typing what it's called: an address, a town or a named spot ("Barrage de Nisramont"). The map moves to a search result you pick. It only shows you the spot; it never adds anything to the trip or your access points.
- **Search result**: A place that place search found, shown with a temporary marker until you clear it. It is not part of the trip and not an access point.
- **On the route**: An access point or official aire within 200 m of the trip's route (the snap distance), measured along the routed line, or the straight line while a leg isn't routed. The Access points tab lists these first in route order, then your other access points nearest to you. Official aires off the route aren't listed.
- **Section**: A run of consecutive legs of the same mode (hike or paddle), ending where the mode changes or at a camp. So a section always falls on one day. It totals its legs and carries all their warnings, but it is only a view of them: you can't name or edit one, and it isn't saved. The GPX export marks where each section starts with a waypoint (ADR 0037). Not the same as a Stretch, which is a legal unit of river.
- **Start date**: The optional calendar date of a trip's first day. Day *n* falls on start date + *n*−1. Access status is checked against these dates.
- **Gauge**: A hydrometric station that measures a river's flow (m³/s). AGW Annex 2 names one per Walloon stretch: its 72-hour mean flow and its flow measured at 08:00 decide whether the stretch is open today. An *assumed* gauge is our best guess at the station Annex 2 means (ADR 0024). An *SPW practice* gauge is the station the SPW really uses where Annex 2's can't be matched (ADR 0035).
- **Snap offer**: A suggestion, made when a leg becomes a paddle leg, a new point makes a put-in or take-out, or you move a put-in or take-out, to move that put-in or take-out onto one of your access points or an official aire nearby. The planner accepts or declines it; the app never moves a point on its own.
- **Base map**: The map drawn under everything else: OpenTopoMap, aerial photos or another topographic map. You pick one at a time; the choice is a viewing preference on your device, never part of the trip. *Avoid*: "layer" for a base map.
- **Overlay**: Anything drawn over the base map that you can switch on or off, such as the access layer or hiking trails. The trip itself is not an overlay.
- **Hiking trails**: The overlay that shows waymarked hiking routes (GR, regional and local walks) from OpenStreetMap, for planning hike legs and portages. *Avoid*: "paths" (every footpath is already on the base map).
- **Selected leg**: The leg you tapped on the map; a card shows its mode, and pressing the other mode changes it (#230). It is never selected together with a point, and any edit but a mode change unselects it.
- **Planner session**: What the planner is working on right now: the trip, plus the selected point or selected leg, snap offers, the next leg's mode (the Hike/Paddle pick for the next leg drawn, until the trip next changes), whether you're in access point editing, your access points with the new one being placed (the draft) and the selected access point, an unanswered question about replacing the trip, and a file error. Only the trip and your access points are saved (the draft only once its details are saved); the rest is gone after a reload.
